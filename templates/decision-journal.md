# Decision Journal Entry

Append-only entries to [notes/decision-journal.md](../notes/decision-journal.md). Borrowed from forecasting practice — the goal is not to record what you decided, but **why**, so future-you can audit the reasoning, not just the outcome.

## Format

```markdown
## [YYYY-MM-DD] <decision>

- **Context:** <one sentence — what was the situation>
- **Options considered:** A, B, C
- **Choice:** <which one>
- **Reasoning:** <2–4 sentences. Be specific. "It seemed right" is not reasoning.>
- **Expected outcome:** <what you expect to happen and by when>
- **Confidence:** 0.6 (calibration target)
- **Could-be-wrong vector:** <the single most likely way this is wrong>
- **Trigger to revisit:** <event or date that would force a re-decision>
- **Cost of being wrong:** low | medium | high
- **Reversible?:** yes | no | partial

### Outcome

_(filled in later)_

- **Date:** YYYY-MM-DD
- **What happened:** <one paragraph>
- **Confidence in retrospect:** <was 0.6 right? high/medium/low>
- **Lesson:** <one sentence — generalizable, not topic-specific>
```

## What counts as a decision worth journaling

Not everything. The bar:

- **Direction changes** — entering a new subfield, switching textbooks, abandoning a problem set.
- **Source bets** — choosing which paper or thinker to trust as a Tier 2 anchor.
- **Stop conditions** — declaring a topic "good enough" for a goal.
- **Method picks** — which proof technique, identification strategy, theoretical framework.
- **Anti-rabbit-hole calls** — explicitly choosing not to learn X.

Routine reading and ingest do not need journal entries.

## Review cadence

Quarterly: read the last 3 months of entries with their outcomes. The question is not "was I right" but "was my confidence calibrated to my reasoning?" Patterns of overconfidence in specific domains are the highest-value signal in this entire system.

Cross-link with `/es-calibrate` for numeric tracking.
